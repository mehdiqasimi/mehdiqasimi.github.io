## About Me

I am a Doctoral Researcher at Tampere University working in the field of cold spray, surface engineering, and materials characterization. My research focuses on inner-diameter cold spray, particle impact behavior, coating characterization, and process optimization.

## Research
My current research focuses on understanding the relationships between cold spray processing conditions, particle behaviour, and coating properties.

<strong>Investigation of In-flight Particles Behavior and Coating Property Correlation for Inner Diameter Cold Spray</strong>

<div class="abstract-text">

<p>
This study explores the Inner Diameter Cold Spray (ID CS) process for deposition of selected feedstock powder materials. The ID CS has mainly its application in coating confined geometries such as bores, pipes and engine cylinders, where conventional cold spray experiences geometrical and technical limitations to perform coating. Three metallic feedstocks were selected: aluminum, copper and nickel-aluminum to evaluate the influence of process parameters such as gas stream pressure, temperature and standoff distance on particle velocity and coating quality.
</p>

<p>
In-flight particle behavior was recorded using diagnostic cameras HiWatch HR2 and CS-Q (Osier Ltd.) to record the effect of shorter nozzle design and the effect of process condition variation on particle acceleration. The coatings were characterized by hardness, microstructural thickness and porosity measurements. Subsequently, the experimental findings showed that particle velocity was most strongly influenced by the gas pressure and standoff distance, while the temperature had a secondary yet a notable role by reducing the material's critical velocity within the selected parameter window.
</p>

<p>
Among the coatings produced using different feedstock materials, the copper feedstock showed the highest coating density and deposition efficiency, while nickel-aluminum coating showed limited build-up due to harder particle nature or likely unsuitable parameter or nozzle inability to accelerate particle with sufficient impact. Aluminum coatings demonstrated sensitivity to standoff distance, revealing the influence of the bow shock effect near the substrate at shorter standoff distance while operated with lower parameter set.
</p>

<p>
The results confirmed that the higher particle velocity was attributed to the stronger plastic deformation and strain hardening, resulting in increased coating hardness and reduced porosity. Substrate hardness also played a role, coatings on the steel substrates achieved higher hardness and slightly denser microstructure compared to that of aluminum.
</p>

<p>
A predictive model based on the response surface methodology was developed using the experimental data for model training which then was used to quantify and correlate the process variables with the coating quality precursors. The quadratic model exhibited strong predictive performance of R² >0.91 (for all responses) and accurately captured both linear and interactive effects of parameters. The model could serve as an effective tool for process parameter optimization in ID CS, facilitating process predictability and minimizing the experimental effort and cost in AM applications.
</p>
